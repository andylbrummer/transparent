# a-night-at-the-north-pole

> Script for the guide "A night at the North Pole". One sentence a line; `caption || spoken` where they differ.
> The guide stands at the pole (90° N) at 2025-12-21 12:00 UTC, the December solstice, then runs the clock six hours.
> At the pole a body's altitude is its declination. Declinations are the app's own ephemeris (packages/core,
> sunGeocentricAu, moonGeocentricKm, planetEphemeris) at that instant: Sun -23.44°, Moon -26.7° and 16° from the Sun,
> Jupiter +21.8° at magnitude -2.6 (Venus, Mercury, Mars and Saturn are below the horizon; Sirius, at -16.7°, too).
> The Sun's declination is below -18° from 14 Nov 2025 to 29 Jan 2026 (same ephemeris): astronomical twilight ends
> at 18°. Polaris: Hipparcos (app/public/sky/stars.json), declination +89.26°. Sidereal day 23 h 56 min 4 s.
> The Sun's declination is negative from the September equinox to the March equinox, so at the pole it is down
> between them; refraction moves sunset and sunrise by a few days, hence "late September" and "March".

## 01-the-pole
You are standing at the North Pole, on the longest night of the year.
Every way you can face from here is south.
The Sun set here in late September, and it will not rise again until March.

## 02-polaris
Look straight up.
The star at the top of the sky is Polaris, the North Star, less than a degree from overhead. || The star at the top of the sky is Polaris, the North Star, less than one degree from overhead.
The Earth's axis points almost straight at it, so from here it hardly moves.

## 03-circling
Now six hours will pass in a few seconds.
Every star circles Polaris, once in 23 hours 56 minutes. || Every star circles Polaris, once in twenty three hours and fifty six minutes.
None of them rises, and none of them sets.
From the pole, the stars above the horizon are up all year, and the rest are never seen.

## 04-jupiter
The brightest point in this sky is the planet Jupiter, 22° above the horizon. || The brightest point in this sky is the planet Jupiter, twenty two degrees above the horizon.
This winter it circles the pole without setting, like the stars.

## 05-the-sun
Now look down through the ground, toward the Sun.
It is 23.4° below the horizon. || It is twenty three point four degrees below the horizon.
Since mid-November it has been more than 18° down, too deep even for twilight, and it stays that deep until late January. || Since mid November it has been more than eighteen degrees down, too deep even for twilight, and it stays that deep until late January.
The Moon is down there too, close to the Sun.
So for weeks on end, the stars have the sky to themselves.
